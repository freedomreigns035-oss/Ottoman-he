KEY CONCEPTS & DEFINITIONS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

MILLET SYSTEM: Ottoman institutional framework allowing religious minorities 
(Christians, Jews) autonomy in governance, law, and tax collection while 
maintaining Ottoman imperial hierarchy. Greek, Armenian, and Jewish millets 
were primary structures.

DEVSHIRME: Forced conscription of Christian boys (20% of conquered Christian 
populations) from 14th century to late 17th century; converts became Ottoman 
military and administrative elite; basis for Janissary corps.

JANISSARIES: Elite Ottoman military force primarily composed of devshirme 
converts; considered among world's most advanced military units during peak years.

SULEIMAN THE MAGNIFICENT (Süleyman Kanuni): 10th Ottoman Sultan (r. 1520–1566); 
considered peak of Ottoman power; earned title "the Lawgiver" for legal reforms; 
established millet system in current form.

KAFES SYSTEM: Post-Suleiman succession method isolating dynastic princes in 
harem without field governance experience; resulted in incompetent sultans and 
accelerated institutional decay.

GRAND VIZIER: Chief minister and administrative head of Ottoman state; increasingly 
held de facto power after Suleiman as sultans withdrew from governance.

BATTLE OF VIENNA (1683): Pivotal conflict where Polish-led coalition defeated 
Ottoman forces under Kara Mustafa; marked end of Ottoman European expansion and 
beginning of four-century decline.

TANZIMAT REFORMS (1839–1876): "Reorganization" period attempting to modernize 
Ottoman institutions along European lines; largely failed due to structural 
incompatibility between Ottoman and European governance models.

ATATÜRK (Mustafa Kemal): Founder and first President of Turkish Republic 
(r. 1923–1938); architect of radical secularization, westernization, and 
modernization; abolished Caliphate, replaced Islamic law with European codes.

KEMALISM: Ideological foundation of Turkish Republic based on Atatürk's "Six 
Arrows": republicanism, nationalism, populism, statism, secularism, and permanent 
revolution; emphasized separation of religion from state governance.

LAÏCITÉ (Laiklik): Turkish secularism principle; enshrined in 1928 constitutional 
amendment removing Islam's official state status; explicitly stated in February 5, 
1937 Constitution.
